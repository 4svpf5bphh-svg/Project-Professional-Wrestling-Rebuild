# PPW — Agent working instructions

## Mission and authority
Project Professional Wrestling (PPW) is an asynchronous, persistent multiplayer wrestling management game. The rebuild exists solely to make the original PPW economical to operate on Neon while preserving agreed gameplay, simulation, content, multiplayer rules and planned UI improvements. Think as a game designer as well as an engineer: TEW underneath, EWR on top.

The owner decides creative direction and release authorization. Implement the approved task, not a speculative redesign. Reuse and adapt existing working code and original gameplay kernels; avoid parallel systems, gratuitous rewrites, repeated planning and documentation for its own sake. Deliver substantial, coherent, playable end-to-end milestones rather than isolated tiny slices. Investigate failures, fix them and rerun relevant tests without stopping for routine progress updates. Stop for genuine blockers, material unresolved design decisions, or actions outside authority. Report remaining gaps honestly.

## Gameplay invariants
- Server-authoritative, deterministic, asynchronous World; two humans must have fair access and no online-first advantage. One weekly lock, 52 weeks per PPW year, restart-safe certification, exact-once accounting and protected private player data. Existing playtest manual Advance Week remains the only advancement path permitted in production unless owner specifically authorizes scheduling.
- Player-scripted winners, finishes and segments are authoritative subject only to established rules (e.g. documented medical stoppage); simulation determines execution and consequences. Undetected cheating is not a DQ. Preserve title eligibility/lineage, real contracts and dates, roster identity, relationships, programmes and staff delegation.
- Respect event identity, weekly or biweekly broadcaster schedules, multi-week booking, separate PPV/TV comparison, multiple same-week events and historical continuity. Do not use AI text to alter certified outcomes.
- Terra is the current preferred results provider per owner direction; Groq is intended for free, reliable editorial/social content. Inspect current implementation for exact provider names, routing, budgets and fallbacks. Never add paid calls, increase caps, expose keys or invoke live providers just to test. Mock providers for tests; preserve bounded retries, stored narratives and grounded wrestling context. Fictional rumours must be clearly in-game, not presented as real-world facts.
- iPhone/PWA first: dense readable screens, accessible controls, contextual pickers rather than long dropdowns, consistent skins/KP branding, meaningful staff advice and vivid wrestling-fluent results. Validate actual interactions and mobile layouts, not aspirational artwork.

## Neon and security
Follow database efficiency contract: bounded authorized indexed projections; no ordinary full-World loads, monolithic rewrites, N+1, or idle polling. Batch queries, enforce transactional revision/idempotency and privacy, preserve deterministic replay and recovery. Target under 5 GB/month transfer, preferably 2.5 GB for two players; distinguish estimated payload from billed transfer and compute. Measure representative query/row/byte budgets and history growth for changes. No production secrets, data exports, live writes, migrations, resets, cron or provider spending in development/audit environments. Use disposable local PostgreSQL and synthetic fixtures; never point tests at live Neon.
