# The Promoter’s Cut
## A premium direction for Project Professional Wrestling

**Decision** Build a game about the consequences of making stars. Use the existing simulation to give players a season they can take credit for, an industry that remembers it, and rivals who have to respond.

**Baseline** Accepted Milestone 7 candidate `8f855d8ff3774c0e811b795d7976aa6d39e26a9b`, independently verified against the draft head. This assessment builds on three authenticated weeks of mobile play and the earlier simulation review. The prototypes are a separate, executable experiment. [Draft PR #146](https://github.com/4svpf5bphh-svg/Project-Professional-Wrestling-Rebuild/pull/146)

## What PPW is fundamentally missing

PPW can settle an outcome and preserve it. It rarely lets a player feel the consequences building before the bell. When a wrestler asks for a promo, the current game can track a deadline and certify whether the request was fulfilled. A premium game should let the choice compete with something the booker also wants. The only time available might be a closing segment the network wants for its top draw. A promise made on air might strengthen the talent’s leverage while making a premium event easier to sell. Putting the wrestler on first fulfils the literal promise and can still disappoint them. The meaning comes from the sacrifice and the audience that saw it.

There is a similar gap at promotion scale. An industry full of active companies does not automatically give the player opponents with intelligible plans. The economic and creative systems need a way to remember the positions companies have taken, what those positions are costing them, and which opportunities are fading. A policy such as building local prospects should change who the crowd forgives, which broadcaster comes calling, and what a powerful rival believes it can take from you.

The current game devotes prominent space to badges, inventories, metrics and destinations. These help a player look things up. Attachment grows when a booker has a prediction, commits to it, watches the audience answer, and deals with a consequence. PPW should make that cycle repeatable in minutes. The ingredients for a hundred-hour save are the times when it fails interestingly and the times when the player proves the industry wrong.

**Premium proposition** A substantive solo game with a recognisable starting promotion, a first season worth finishing, and a save that can develop its own history. Independently owned multiplayer extends the same proposition. A friend can compare the season from their side of a title fight, negotiation or ratings war. A buyer should see both the freedom to book their match and the reason the next week will be different.

## New mechanics, in priority order

Effort bands are estimates for an experienced team using the current implementation, inclusive of focused design, engineering and QA. Small is roughly 1–2 person-weeks, medium 3–6, and large 7–12. The estimates exclude general production polish, asset licensing, localisation and device QA. Tuning across multiple seasons is the major uncertainty. All proposals fit the existing hosting model and require **zero new routine external AI calls**.

| Priority | Mechanic | The decision it creates | Estimated complexity and running cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 · Essential | **Public commitments with audience witnesses** | Promise a title opportunity on camera to sell a match the network needs. Break the promise and the wrestler gains leverage in public. Offer a qualifying bout, reschedule around injury, or let staff privately negotiate a credible substitute. A person can remember how you handled the problem after the news cycle ends. | **Large.** Build on existing private promises with one active public commitment per story, a certified segment reference, a due week and one settlement. Reuse the Backstage pass and public facts. Disclose private concessions only to the appropriate actor. |
| 2 · Essential | **Audiences remember what they paid to see** | One territory rewards competitive wrestling, another returns for homegrown stars, and a national slot values ongoing stories. Two consecutive shows with the same aggregate score can build different followings. Changing identity risks the audience you already earned. | **Large.** Track at most three aggregated tastes per active territory, updated from the existing certified card and market batch. Use coarse stickiness and decay with hard bounds. Fold small updates into current cadence and eliminate any per-fan simulation. |
| 3 · Essential | **Rivals commit to plans they can abandon** | A rival with a threatened slot says it will double down for two weeks. You can challenge it directly, book another day, undercut it in a territory, or wait for the gamble to fail. Its public moves and signed obligations give enough evidence to make a choice. | **Medium–large.** One bounded intent per AI promotion, with a time limit and a few deterministic triggers. Choose or revise it from existing public, certified context. Private drafts and plans remain private. No replanning loop or extra polling. |
| 4 · Essential | **Career bets that change what a prospect can do** | Put your name behind a prospect. Choose a veteran partner, a protected TV role or a tougher route with faster learning and higher exposure to failure. The veteran also has a career to protect. A breakthrough opens a new decision about pay, dates and the title picture. | **Large.** At most a small active cohort per promotion. Derive progress from certified appearances, opponent experience, useful failures and existing skills. Settle changes in the ordinary career batch. Generate a small annual intake without requiring the global talent population to shrink first. |
| 5 · High value | **Locker-room coalitions with something to lose** | A trusted faction veteran agrees to elevate a newcomer if their tag partner keeps a spot. Talent may refuse a short-notice loss, accept a protected match, or split their loyalty when the manager repeats a slight. | **Large.** Keep one active motive per featured wrestler and use existing teams and working relationships. Generate a negotiation only when an existing relationship or commitment would actually change. Cap inbox volume and settle a few meaningful state changes from certified evidence. |
| 6 · High value | **Promotion founding charters and season pivots** | Pick a clear opening challenge. Keep a regional venue afloat, build a women-led television product, or turn an expensive inherited roster into a touring attraction. At a season break, choose whether to keep that identity or risk a pivot. | **Medium–large.** Author a few Genesis starts as fixture presets using existing promotion, market and roster fields. Recalculate the smallest required identity summary at certified season checkpoints. Use the same systems in solo and shared Worlds. |
| 7 · High value | **History that can be reclaimed** | A forgotten champion returns because their record is about to fall. A second-generation prospect can keep, break or reinterpret a parent’s legacy. A title tournament can settle a disputed lineage or bring back a territory’s signature event. | **Medium.** Trigger only from pre-existing title, career and event records. Pin the relevant facts when the situation becomes active. Page the rest via existing archive indexes. No archival scans on the ordinary Home screen. |
| 8 · Optional | **Limited co-promotion agreements** | Trade dates or plan one shared event without giving up control of a company. Compete for the same wrestler commercially while agreeing on what happens on the shared card. | **Large.** Two explicit approvals, bounded agreed rights and a single certified settlement. Existing resource arbitration and player isolation stay authoritative. Defer until solo commitments and ordinary two-manager seasons are stable. |

A useful distinction is between **private assurance**, **public promise** and **contract term**. They have different audiences, consequences and remedies. Preserve the existing private promise and contract logic. Add the public exposure only when the player authors an action on screen that warrants it. A default, one-week contact or flavour message should never create an obligation accidentally.

### Make surprise legible

The booker controls the scripted result. The simulation decides what the result means. Before a show, display known pressure, plausible direction and binding constraints. After a show, name the evidence behind two material changes. A prospect winning and getting booed can be more interesting than an automatic success if the player understands what the crowd wanted and has a response next week.

Track achievements as moments that changed the save. An inaugural reign, a real break-even comeback, a trainee who pulls a rival’s rating, or a sellout after a cancelled main event belongs in the season account. A string of collection unlocks has little bearing on whether the player wants to book the next show.

### Wrestling moments worth producing

| Moment | Presentation and next decision |
|---|---|
| First championship or genuinely historic upset | Give the result a one-time reveal, a named reaction and permanent lineage. Ask who earns the first defence. The title art and winner remain available immediately through a quick result. |
| An injury knocks out the advertised main event | Show the verified clearance and affected promises on the card. Offer two lawful roster replacements with different creative and cost consequences, plus the ability to cast freely. |
| A hometown comeback goes against the script’s intended reaction | Show the crowd response and why the planned heel or face reaction failed. Give the booker the option to follow the audience, quiet the angle or stay the course. |
| A rival takes your star or TV slot | Give the player one specific headline with the certified change, an immediate choice about the next show, and a public record both managers can point to. |
| A veteran leaves something behind | Present a short end-of-career vignette using one certified bout and one recorded relationship. Offer a staff role, a last match if eligible, or a simple retirement. Keep the archive even if the player skips the vignette. |

## Premium visual and interaction direction

The proposition is a **promoter’s production desk by day and a wrestling broadcast at night**. Organise scenes around what a booker is doing rather than around which table supplied the data. The same cast, venue and event identity should carry across the week, the card, the result and the archive.

- **Arrival and Home.** Start on a named episode with a venue, one recognisable conflict and the next action. Later starts can vary the lead prospect, title and stake. Metadata supports the scene. Established players return to their last active decision instead of sitting through an introduction.
- **The card.** Use a compact run sheet with a contextual closing-segment decision and an inline forecast. Offer one-tap reuse of regular structure and staff preparation. Keep the deep match and angle editor discoverable for bespoke work. Card order, finish, cast, chemistry, injuries and rights are productive complexity. Re-entering budgets and calendar defaults is administration.
- **The arena.** Distinguish the room with sparse text, a different composition and previously approved event and belt art. Use one short optional introduction for an important show, one curtain reveal, and a direct result beside it. Let an opening cue or chime mark an intentional action on devices that support audio. No autoplay, recurring soundtrack or mandatory wait.
- **After the bell.** Place the image or line people will remember above the relevant result metrics, then give one next step. The full recap, commercial breakdown and press scrum remain available on demand. A title change deserves a different visual treatment from an attendance report.
- **Dossiers and backstage.** Lead with the character’s current want, who matters to them and the last moment that changed the relationship. Put book, negotiate and review rights within reach. Make private conversations visually quieter than the arena. Put the complete scouting numbers and historical record one level deeper.
- **The season.** Show what changed and which choice the player still owns. Use a timeline of fights, promises, splits, turnarounds and title changes drawn from certified history. Surface provenance and private information boundaries as part of the data design.
- **Navigation, language and feedback.** Use short verbs at the point of action and familiar mobile controls. Pair status colour with text, preserve focus on return and make failures explain the remedy. Replace suspense loaders with a truthful confirmation state. Reserve the largest motion and typography for moments that warrant them.

The prototype uses a small display serif for episode titles and system sans for decisions, scores and controls. Amber means an authored action or season consequence, paper highlights an immediate decision, and arena lighting distinguishes the broadcast. Accessible reduced motion keeps the same content and direct entry points. At 320px the compact card still exposes its two decisions. Bigger text, safe areas, native screens, screen-reader speech and touch reach need testing on physical iPhone hardware before shipping. ## What the running prototypes demonstrate

The connected preview is implemented in **PPW’s TypeScript, CSS, HTML and Vite stack**, using the shipped PPW wordmark, intro backdrop and generic women’s championship artwork. The Week 3 title, participants, PPV buys and attendance come from the earlier disposable, certified browser run. Week 4 and its branching outcomes are a fixture. The head-to-head audience calculation calls the production `projectRatingsWarWeek` function. The prototype uses one scoped browser storage entry. It has no account, backend route or production data write.

| Prototype | What actually works |
|---|---|
| **This Week** | Start on Ivy’s first defence, read Coastline’s declared intent, open Ivy’s dossier, and start booking from the main choice. |
| **The Run Sheet** | Choose the defence or a PPV build. Airtime and event cost update immediately. Open the optional controls to script the winner or a draw and reorder segments. The fixture explains the pacing consequence of moving the featured segment from the last slot. |
| **Ringside** | Confirm, see the curtain, then reveal or skip directly to the result. The report follows the authored result. Another promotion wins or loses the shared slot and responds accordingly. The 390px recording shows Vera winning the belt. The 320px alternate route keeps Ivy as champion and gives Vera a public claim to the next match. |
| **Season and Dossier** | See the new or retained reign, save or reopen a future commitment, and open the relevant wrestler dossier. The result and next arrangement survive refresh. Dossiers support Escape and restore the invoking focus. |

Two decisions have substantive effects within the fixture. Booking a defence brings $15,000 extra event cost and stronger TV performance. The authored finish determines who carries the belt into Week 6. Holding the match preserves the champion and gives Vera a public claim, while Coastline retains the shared slot after winning the head-to-head. The player can follow through, or leave the contender slot open and see the unresolved challenge in the season. The test demonstrates consequences across screens. The next premium event and the larger proposed motive and audience models are design work beyond this episode.

The mobile evidence records a real click-through of both branches, including the scripted title switch, pacing warning, reopen and save, local persistence, reduced motion, dossier keyboard navigation, and the option to skip the reveal. The prototype’s clearest remaining visual limitation is the absence of portraits for these fictional wrestlers. The existing belt and graphic assets can carry one scene. A full premium release needs a small, approved set of distinctive character assets, readable silhouettes and role-specific pose or treatment. Confirm rights before putting any existing artwork into a commercial product.

## What to take from other commercial games

- **Into the Breach** previews enemy attacks so the player can reason about an outcome before committing. Apply that legibility to known network obligations, availability, published rival plans and medical deadlines. Preserve uncertainty about the crowd and private intent. [Subset Games](https://www.subsetgames.com/itb.html)
- **Crusader Kings III** lets personality and longer-term ambitions influence what characters do. Give PPW’s wrestlers a few motives that matter to a specific decision and a memory of how the booker treated them. A cast of interchangeable reactions defeats the point. [Paradox](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/crusader-kings-iii/about)
- **Hades** makes returns to the same places interesting through changed relationships and responses to prior play. A Backstage visit after a failed push should offer a fresh response to a known person. The budget-friendly equivalent is tightly scoped state and a few authored reactions per situation. [Supergiant Games](https://www.supergiantgames.com/blog/hades-faq/)
- **Journey of Wrestling** shows the value of quick browser booking and accessible creative choice. The comparison test for PPW is whether someone can get to a satisfying first show more quickly, then have more reasons to care about the next month. [Journey of Wrestling](https://www.journeyofwrestling.com/)
- **Football Manager** makes a complicated cast navigable through roles, planning and focused advice. PPW can put one actionable person or cost beside the existing booking choice, so the player sees why the deeper system is there. [Sports Interactive](https://www.footballmanager.com/features/recruitment-revamp)

## Operating envelope and implementation approach

- **Database and integrity.** Make one compact read model for the current week’s playable decisions using indexed sources and the existing certified boundary. Project consequences when World settlement already has the relevant rows. Index active and due items by World, owning promotion and week. Keep a bounded number of current decisions and page history. Public stories and authenticated owner-only commitments use their existing scopes. Reuse command receipts for retries and never let a frontend prototype calculate production settlement.
- **External AI.** Use deterministic state and authored building blocks for mechanical consequences. Editorial flavour can consume only already authorised capacity. Key reuse by certified fact digest, and keep optional generation outside confirmation and opening the next week. Sharper lines on a few meaningful events have more value than more text on every result.
- **Frontend transfer.** The built preview emits one JS, one CSS and the required existing SVG. The logo and backdrop are included by the existing bundler’s small-asset handling. No font service, video playback, external images, polling, service worker or live requests are involved. The evidence video is a repository artifact. Production integration should load arena presentation on demand and budget first-route transfer separately from cumulative portrait or archive reads.
- **Battery and motion.** Use static art, a 220 ms reveal on a single interaction, motion preference support, and opt-in audio from a short Web Audio cue that closes its context. Use no idle animation loop and no persistent audio. Native iOS memory, battery, frame pacing and browser restoration are device-test requirements. Desktop simulation of CPU and network measures a local reference point.
- **Operating gates.** Before a feature graduates from the experiment, compare query count, bytes and logical rows changed per certified week at 2, 8 and a larger World size. The earlier eight-company run measured 750–1,109 SQL statements across sampled weeks, so added narration and memory must be paid for by sharing inputs and compact writes. Start with a bounded diff, then run the fixture twice to verify idempotency and ownership. Use no scheduler or added paid services.

## Roadmap towards a game worth buying

1. **Vertical slice.** Put the first-show path, a meaningful late-card decision and the result-to-next-week handoff into an isolated, real-settlement playtest. Fix the known broadcast-day client defect alongside it. Run cold mobile sessions and measure completion, time to a first result and whether players can correctly explain one consequence without help.
2. **A first season.** Ship an authored 13-week solo trial with distinct founding charters, one public commitment system, clear finances and a credible crisis recovery. Confirm that the game accommodates at least two viable approaches, with setbacks that can be explained and acted on in time.
3. **The wrestling industry pushes back.** Add audience memory and rival plans together. Use scenarios with the same opening conditions and different manager actions. Observe different rival decisions, title arcs and player reasons to continue. Grow career bets without expanding the busywork on every card.
4. **Commercial polish.** Finish a coherent visual and sound language for select moments, native device accessibility, deliberate onboarding and a replayable archive. A week should make sense at a glance and be completable in a short phone session. Keep granular editing for the bookers who want it.
5. **Optional expansion.** Once both solo and ordinary shared seasons hold up, consider costed co-promotion, selected historical starts and deeper season keepsakes. Evaluate each against repeat play, creative freedom and the existing operating envelope.

**Avoid** a screen for every new mechanic, per-wrestler task lists, constant notifications, long unskippable ceremonies, a compulsive collection grind, and paid calls that generate routine matches or change the simulation’s truth. Avoid a universal best card or an identity that functions as a permanent stat bonus. A player who chooses a regional style should face different problems from a national producer. Maintain the existing security and certification architecture.

PPW should sell the feeling of being the person who saw a star before the rest of wrestling did. A player should remember why they risked the closing segment, who objected, how the crowd answered, and what they did about it the following week. A friend running the rival promotion should remember it for a different reason.
